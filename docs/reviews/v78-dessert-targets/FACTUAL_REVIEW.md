## `contexto[0]` — Cozonac — factual accept

The target is an ordinary Romanian food concept. DEX editions define it as a sweet preparation made from leavened dough with eggs, milk, butter, sugar and other ingredients. The same lexicographic page carries definitions and usage across CADE (1926–1931), DLRLC (1955–1957), DEX 1998/2009 and DOOM 3 (2021), supporting durable cross-generational recognition rather than a recent media spike. A 2025 nationally broadcast PRO TV `La Măruță` contest devoted multiple daily rounds and a final to “Cozonacul de aur”; that is an independent current prime-time/general-audience signal.

The record’s identity and description are materially sound. A five-generation recipe published by PRO TV uses flour, sugar, butter, egg yolks, milk and yeast, fills the dough with walnut and cocoa, and explicitly connects the family preparation to both Easter and Christmas. AGERPRES independently documents a Moldavian cozonac recipe with flour, walnut, cocoa and alternatives including rahat, poppy seed or raisins. The wording “nelipsit” should be read as the familiar festive convention, not a literal claim that every Romanian household serves it at both holidays.

Dominant sense is acceptable. Dexonline lists the fixed argot expression `a dospi cozonacul` (“to idle”), but it does not establish a competing standalone nominal referent. In isolation, and especially under `gastronomie`, `Cozonac` points to the food.

## `contexto[1]` — Clătite — factual accept

The target is also an ordinary Romanian food concept. DLRLC (1955–1957) defines `clătită` as a thin fried preparation filled with jam, cheese or vegetables and includes literary usage, providing a historical recognition signal. AGERPRES independently describes the common batter as flour, eggs, sugar, milk and melted butter, with sweet fillings such as jam/chocolate and savory fillings such as mushrooms or cheese. Current national-TV use is equally direct: a 2025 PRO TV daytime show prepared thin pan-cooked sheets from flour, milk and eggs in both savory and jam-filled versions. These independent historical, national-media and current-broadcast signals comfortably establish recognition of the dish.

The record description—thin cooked batter sheets, usually filled sweet or savory—is factually supported. The direct flour and egg ingredient routes and associations with jam, cheese and ice cream are ordinary. Dessert peers such as Papanași, Plăcinte and Gogoși are category associations rather than identity claims.

There is a grammatical ambiguity but no strong target polysemy. `Clătite` can be the feminine plural participial/adjectival form of `a clăti` (“rinsed”), as in a phrase with an expressed noun. As a standalone capitalized label under `gastronomie`, its dominant nominal reading is the food. This should still be challenged at the later gameplay review if uncontextualized input behavior creates an actual collision; no second standalone entity is evidenced here.
